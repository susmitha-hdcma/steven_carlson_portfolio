01 · Healthcare technology / EMS operations

CADMedic.AI

FOUNDER · ARCHITECT · ENGINEER

An AI-integrated operations platform for private EMS agencies, designed around one connected transport record—from the initial request through field care, operational coordination, and revenue.

Healthcare workflowsAI-assisted operationsiOS field appSecurity by design

The challenge

Private EMS operations span several connected but distinct workflows: receiving a transport request, dispatching the right unit, recording patient care, coordinating staff and vehicles, managing medication custody, and completing billing. When those workflows live in disconnected systems, teams can face duplicated entry, fragmented operational visibility, and difficult handoffs.

The platform therefore needed to serve real operational conditions—not just provide a dashboard. Field teams may have limited connectivity, sensitive patient information requires careful handling, and medication custody needs a traceable record.

Product and architecture approach

  • Organize dispatch, electronic patient-care reporting (ePCR), billing and revenue-cycle work, fleet management, scheduling, facility requests, and medication custody around a shared transport record.
  • Provide a facility request portal so requests can enter the operational workflow in a structured way.
  • Include an offline-capable iOS field workflow with synchronization for situations where connectivity is unreliable.
  • Apply AI-assisted capabilities to chart-quality review, narrative-driven revenue capture, availability prediction, and route or unit optimization.

Security and trust boundaries

  • Design for protection of protected health information (PHI) and role-based access, so users access information appropriate to their responsibilities.
  • Use auditable records to support review of important actions and changes.
  • Represent controlled-medication custody as a traceable chain of custody rather than an informal note.
  • Keep privacy, authorization, and auditability as core product concerns alongside usability and speed.

These are design intentions and stated platform features; they should not be read as an independent certification or compliance audit.

Engineering priorities

The core design challenge is keeping related operational modules consistent. A transport record should connect the work rather than force each department to maintain a separate version of the same event. Offline use introduces another concern: local field activity must be synchronized without losing the context needed to understand the final record.

01 / Workflow

Connect request, dispatch, field documentation, fleet, scheduling, and billing.

02 / Field reality

Support mobile work and offline-capable use where network availability may vary.

03 / Accountability

Build access controls, auditability, PHI safeguards, and medication traceability into the workflow.

Outcome / stated product direction

A connected operations platform built around one transport record from request to revenue. Quantified time savings, revenue improvement, user adoption, and clinical outcomes should be added only when measured and approved for publication.

02 · AI systems / developer security

WaymarkHQ

FOUNDER · ARCHITECT · ENGINEER

A governance control plane for AI coding agents, placing a deliberate boundary between what an agent proposes and what it is allowed to execute.

AI agent governanceLeast privilegePolicy enforcementDevSecOps

The challenge

AI coding agents can read repositories, modify files, run commands, and participate in delivery workflows. Those capabilities can accelerate engineering, but they also raise questions: Which credentials can an agent reach? Which actions require human approval? How can changes be isolated, reviewed, and traced? How does a team recover when an automated change is not safe to release?

Simply asking an agent to “be careful” is not an enforcement boundary. The system needs controls around the agent, not only instructions inside its prompt.

Solution structure

  • A native macOS Studio gives developers a consolidated view of agents and isolated workspaces.
  • A Broker layer keeps credentials outside the agent's direct reach and evaluates policy before execution.
  • Isolated Git worktrees help separate parallel or experimental work from the primary working tree.
  • Approval gates and release checks make important transitions visible and reviewable.
  • MCP integration supports agent-connected workflows within a governed architecture.

Security model

  • Least privilege: do not give an agent broader access than its task needs.
  • Policy before action: evaluate an operation before allowing a privileged action to proceed.
  • Credential separation: reduce direct exposure of secrets to autonomous processes.
  • Traceability: preserve evidence of decisions and actions for later review.
  • Human accountability: make approvals and release decisions explicit where required.

Why this matters

AI governance becomes more useful when it is part of the developer workflow instead of a separate policy document. By placing checks at the point where an agent attempts an action, the approach aims to make safe behavior enforceable and reviewable while preserving a productive development loop.

Waymark's stated design includes recovery tooling such as RepoSeatbelt, isolated workspaces, and release controls. Specific effectiveness claims require test results or production evidence.

TRUST BOUNDARY

Agent intent is separated from privileged execution and credentials.

CONTROL POINTS

Policy checks, approvals, isolated workspaces, and release gates.

EVIDENCE

Keep a reviewable trail of important actions and decisions.

Outcome / stated product direction

A governance layer intended to help teams adopt AI coding agents with stronger control over permissions, execution, and release. Add benchmark results, supported integrations, or customer outcomes only when verified.

03 · Computer vision / online-safety investigations

Cyberbully Watch

FOUNDER · ARCHITECT · ACQUIRED 2020

A computer-vision and machine-learning platform built to support online-safety investigations, with attention to ingestion at scale and preserving the integrity of evidence.

Computer visionMachine learningData pipelinesEvidence integrity

The challenge

Investigative work involving online imagery can require collecting large amounts of data, finding relevant visual matches, and presenting results in a way that can be reviewed. A useful tool must do more than return a similarity result: the surrounding process needs clear records and controls so that investigators can understand how material was collected and handled.

The engineering challenge combined high-volume ingestion, image matching, operational security, and evidence traceability.

System approach

  • Build crawling and ingestion pipelines to bring online material into a processing workflow.
  • Apply computer-vision and machine-learning techniques to image matching.
  • Use Python and Perl in the data-processing and pipeline work.
  • Design verifiable logging and tamper-resistant audit trails to support review of how evidence was handled.

Security and evidentiary integrity

  • Use role-based access control to limit access to sensitive investigative workflows.
  • Keep records that help establish the sequence of collection and processing actions.
  • Use VPN-segmented access as part of the security approach.
  • Make evidence integrity and chain of custody explicit engineering concerns rather than afterthoughts.

The presence of these design elements does not, by itself, establish legal admissibility or certification; those determinations depend on the actual implementation and relevant procedures.

Delivery and transition

The venture moved from concept into production and was acquired in 2020. Steven led knowledge transfer and handoff after the acquisition. That transition is part of the engineering story: systems need to be understandable and transferable beyond the original builder.

INGEST

Collect and process material through structured pipelines.

ANALYZE

Use computer vision and machine learning for image matching.

TRACE

Preserve access controls, logs, and chain-of-custody context.

Outcome / documented milestone

Cyberbully Watch reached production and was acquired in 2020; post-acquisition knowledge transfer was part of the handoff. No unverified accuracy, volume, or investigation-success metrics are claimed here.

04 · Enterprise SaaS / cloud security

stockNum Systems / AutoCorner

CO-FOUNDER · BOARD MEMBER · CISO

Security and infrastructure leadership for a nationwide automotive-dealership SaaS platform, balancing operational reliability, sensitive data protection, and enterprise customer expectations.

Enterprise SaaSAWSPCI DSSRisk managementIncident response

The operating context

As a co-founder and board member, Steven helped build a SaaS business serving automotive dealerships and worked in a CISO-equivalent role. The platform operated in an enterprise environment where customer trust, payment-related requirements, personal information, availability, and vendor expectations were important to long-term relationships.

The portfolio describes the business as supporting more than 3,000 dealerships and protecting millions of personally identifiable information (PII) records. These are portfolio-stated scale figures, not independently audited measurements in this case study.

Infrastructure modernization

  • Led migration from on-premises Red Hat Enterprise Linux (RHEL) infrastructure toward a hardened AWS architecture.
  • Used segmented virtual private clouds (VPCs) and VPN access as part of the infrastructure security approach.
  • Focused on infrastructure hardening, monitoring, and threat detection to support the production environment.
  • Considered security architecture as an operational system that needs ongoing ownership, not a one-time deployment.

Security operations and governance

  • Supported security governance, incident response, and investigation processes.
  • Addressed vendor risk and audit readiness as part of enterprise security operations.
  • Worked toward PCI DSS compliance and protection of sensitive customer information.
  • Connected technical controls with business requirements and enterprise customer expectations.

Leadership perspective

Long-running SaaS security requires coordination across architecture, operations, business leadership, and external partners. The work is not only selecting a control; it is ensuring the control has an owner, can be monitored, and can be explained during an incident or audit.

The co-founder role also meant that engineering decisions had to fit the realities of building and operating a business over time.

ARCHITECTURE

Move toward hardened cloud infrastructure with segmented network boundaries.

OPERATIONS

Maintain monitoring, threat detection, incident response, and clear ownership.

ASSURANCE

Support PCI DSS, vendor risk, audit readiness, and enterprise trust.

Outcome / business and security context

Security leadership and infrastructure practices supported a nationwide SaaS platform and its long-term enterprise relationships. The portfolio states support for 3,000+ dealerships and protection of millions of PII records; no breach-reduction or financial-impact figures are asserted here.

A repeatable way of working

How I approach high-stakes engineering

Across product building, AI governance, investigative technology, and enterprise security, the same operating principles recur.

01 / DISCOVER

Understand the work

Map stakeholders, real-world workflows, sensitive data, dependencies, and failure conditions before choosing a design.

02 / DESIGN

Define boundaries

Make permissions, trust boundaries, auditability, recovery, and ownership part of the architecture.

03 / DELIVER

Build for operation

Favor connected workflows, incremental delivery, reviewable changes, and systems teams can maintain.

04 / VERIFY

Substantiate outcomes

Document decisions, validate controls, and distinguish measured outcomes from goals or design intent.
